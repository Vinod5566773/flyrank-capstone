Refresh Opportunity Scoring for Search Content Performance

FlyRank ML Internship Capstone

1. Abstract

Can recent search-performance signals identify content experiencing meaningful traffic decline and help prioritize content for review or refresh? The analysis defines decline using a 20% or greater reduction in impressions between consecutive 30-day periods and evaluates a 200-tree Random Forest using search-performance features. On a random row split, the model achieved 65.6% accuracy compared with a 63.3% majority-class baseline, with a declining-class F1 score of 0.756. On an unseen-client split, accuracy was 61.1% versus a 67.7% baseline, while adding kept impressions increased accuracy to 66.3% and declining-class F1 to 0.745. These results support using the analysis as a review-prioritization aid, but they do not establish strong unseen-client generalization or any causal effect of content refreshes.

2. Question

Can recent search-performance signals identify content experiencing meaningful traffic decline and help prioritize content for review or refresh?

3. Data

This project uses the FlyRank ML Internship search-intelligence warehouse, accessed through the gated Hugging Face release. The analysis aggregates daily search-performance data into content-level 30-day windows. The final model and ranking dataset contains 111,247 content items with sufficient history, using a minimum of 100 impressions in the previous 30-day period.

The main signals include previous-period impressions, visible-query activity, rare-query share, anonymous-query share, top-query share, and kept impressions. A 90-day feature-engineering exercise was also tested as a robustness check, but the reported model comparison uses the final 30-day feature table.

Only aggregate results are presented in this public report. Client names, domains, private queries, URLs, credentials, and raw client-level exports are excluded.

4. Methodology

A content item is labelled as declining when impressions in the most recent 30-day period are less than 80% of impressions in the preceding 30-day period. The main model uses previous-period impressions, visible-query activity, rare-query share, anonymous-query share, and top-query share as features.

A second model adds kept impressions to test whether this signal improves performance on unseen clients. A 200-tree Random Forest classifier was evaluated using both a stratified random row split and a client-held-out split. Majority-class accuracy was used as the baseline.

For prioritization, an opportunity score combines observed traffic decline with normalized kept impressions. Rule-based action categories are then used for investigation, refresh, monitoring, or maintenance. These scores and actions are operational prioritization heuristics rather than causal claims.

5. Results

Validation Accuracy Declining-class F1 Baseline Accuracy
Random row split 65.6% 0.756 63.3%
Client-held-out split 61.1% 0.704 67.7%
Client-held-out + kept impressions 66.3% 0.745 67.7%

The random row split achieved 65.6% accuracy compared with a 63.3% majority-class baseline. On the stricter client-held-out split, accuracy decreased to 61.1% compared with a 67.7% baseline. Adding kept impressions improved the client-held-out result to 66.3% accuracy and 0.745 declining-class F1, although accuracy remained below the majority baseline.

The resulting rule-based prioritization categories contain 42,756 items for review or refresh, 39,700 for maintain/monitor, 27,631 for monitoring declining performance, and 1,160 for investigating traffic loss.

6. Limitations

  • The client-held-out evaluation does not outperform the majority-class baseline on accuracy, so generalization to unseen clients is not established.
  • Adding kept impressions improved the declining-class F1 score and accuracy relative to the basic client-held-out model, but accuracy remained below the baseline.
  • The opportunity score is a heuristic and can strongly prioritize content with zero recent impressions, requiring human review before action.
  • The action categories are operational rules rather than causal recommendations.
  • The analysis does not establish that observed declines were caused by search-engine algorithm changes or that refreshing content would cause traffic to recover.
  • The anonymized dataset limits the contextual information available for diagnosing individual content items.

7. Ranked Recommendations

  1. Investigate traffic loss: prioritize content with zero impressions in the most recent 30-day period.
  2. Review or refresh content: prioritize content with an observed impression decline of 50% or more.
  3. Monitor declining performance: track content with an impression decline of 20% to 50%.
  4. Maintain / monitor: continue monitoring content that does not meet the decline thresholds.

This ordering is an operational workflow for reviewing the dataset, not a model-proven ranking of business value. Individual items should be reviewed using additional context before any content changes are made.
